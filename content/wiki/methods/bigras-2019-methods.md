# Bigras et al. (2019) — Methods

**Summary**: Within-subject laboratory experiment (N = 20 professional retail assortment planners) comparing three recommendation agent presentation conditions varying transparency and cognitive effort, with eye-tracking and self-report measures, finding that transparent low-cognitive-effort presentation maximized decision quality, sense of control, and satisfaction.

**Sources**: Bigras et al._ Recommendation agent adoption.pdf

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1 / RQ2 / RQ3

**Last updated**: 2026-04-19

---

## Study design
experimental (within-subject laboratory experiment with counterbalanced conditions)

## Sample
- N: 20
- Population: domain professionals (retail assortment planners; mean age 26; 11 men, 9 women)
- Domain: retail / organizational (assortment planning decisions for fictitious clothing retail scenarios)
- Country / region: not reported
- Recruitment method: convenience (professional sample of retail planners)

## Transparency operationalization
- Type: algorithmic transparency (disclosure of recommendation reasoning) and process transparency (showing reasoning steps)
- Manipulation or measure: within-subjects manipulation of three recommendation agent (RA) conditions counterbalanced across two scenarios — (T1) non-transparent RA with low cognitive effort: recommendations provided without explanation; (T2) transparent RA with low cognitive effort: explanations for each recommendation displayed in a modal window accessible at point of recommendation; (T3) transparent RA with high cognitive effort: same explanations as T2 but requiring navigation to a separate page. Transparency content was identical in T2 and T3; accessibility and effort required differed. Prototype RA built with Axure RP 8.
- Scale / instrument name: not applicable (experimental manipulation via interface design)
- Number of items: not applicable

## Trust operationalization
- Type: cognitive (source credibility — trustworthiness and expertise dimensions); behavioural (adoption intention; eye-tracking fixation behavior)
- Measure: source credibility assessed via self-report scale measuring trustworthiness and expertise dimensions of the RA; behavioral trust operationalized via intention to adopt the RA (self-report) and eye-tracking fixation counts and gaze duration on recommendations and explanations (Smart Eye Pro, 60 Hz); sense of control and decision satisfaction measured as self-report scales; objective decision quality measured as accuracy against a pre-determined optimal assortment
- Scale / instrument name: source credibility scale (trustworthiness and expertise dimensions; specific scale name not reported); custom satisfaction and sense-of-control items
- Number of items: not reported
- Behavioural vs self-report: both (eye-tracking fixations and actual decision quality as behavioral; source credibility, adoption intention, sense of control, satisfaction as self-report)

## Moderators and covariates tested
- Cognitive effort to access explanations (low vs. high): operationalized by T2 vs. T3 design; moderated whether transparent RA produced objective decision quality benefits — T2 outperformed T3 on quality despite both being transparent
- Note: user expertise was NOT explicitly varied; all participants were professional assortment planners of comparable experience level. The authors note that the organizational mandate context (employees, not consumers) represents a role-based moderation of adoption dynamics (relevant to RQ3 as an implicit role effect).

## Statistical approach
- Primary analysis method: within-subjects repeated-measures ANOVA or equivalent (specific method not detailed in source summary); qualitative post-task interviews analyzed thematically
- Software: not reported
- Key model fit or effect size reported: significance levels reported for pairwise comparisons (T1 vs. T2, T2 vs. T3, T1 vs. T3) on each dependent variable; specific effect sizes not reported in source summary

## Author-noted limitations
- Very small sample size (N = 20) severely limits statistical power and generalizability
- Within-subject design with two scenarios may produce carryover effects despite counterbalancing
- Participants were professional planners from a single industry; generalizability to other organizational contexts or expertise levels is uncertain
- The prototype RA (Axure RP 8) may not reflect the usability characteristics of real deployed recommendation systems
- Cognitive effort was operationalized as interface navigation rather than explanation complexity; other forms of cognitive demand are not tested
- Qualitative desire for customizable RA (9/20 participants) suggests important individual differences not captured by the experimental design
