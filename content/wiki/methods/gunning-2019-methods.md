# Gunning & Aha 2019 — Methods

**Summary**: Programmatic framework paper describing DARPA's four-year XAI initiative, its technical strategies, and a psychologically grounded evaluation framework for explanation effectiveness; not an empirical study.

**Sources**: Gunning & Aha_DARPA's explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) program.pdf

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1, RQ2

**Last updated**: 2026-04-19

## Transparency operationalization
- Type: taxonomic — deep explanation (modified deep learning), interpretable models (rule-based / causal), and model induction (post-hoc surrogate methods such as LIME)
- Manipulation or measure: conceptual framework proposing three strategies for improving explainability; no empirical manipulation
- Scale / instrument name: not applicable
- Number of items: not applicable

## Trust operationalization
- Type: appropriate trust (calibrated reliance); contrasted explicitly with over-trust (automation bias) and under-trust (algorithm aversion)
- Measure: the IHMC psychological evaluation framework proposes four measurable categories: user satisfaction ratings, mental model accuracy/completeness, task performance, and appropriate trust/reliance — but these are proposed for future experiments, not measured in this paper
- Scale / instrument name: not applicable
- Number of items: not applicable
- Behavioural vs self-report: not applicable

## Moderators and covariates tested

## Author-noted limitations
- Phase 1 empirical results from the XAI evaluations are only briefly referenced; full empirical evidence was forthcoming at time of publication
- The performance–explainability trade-off remains a fundamental challenge that the program aims to address but does not fully resolve
- Generalisation of the evaluation framework beyond the two challenge domains (data analytics, autonomy) is not established
