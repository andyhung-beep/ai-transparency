# Chu 2023 — Methods

**Summary**: A between-subjects Wizard-of-Oz experiment (N = 42) examining how AI model description framing (basic vs. advanced) affects user agreement, behavioural alignment, and trust in an AI job interview training system.

**Sources**: Chu et al._User judgement of an AI model.pdf

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1 / RQ2 / RQ3

**Last updated**: 2026-04-19

---

## Study design
experimental (between-subjects Wizard-of-Oz)

## Sample
- N: 42
- Population: students (recruited from computer science courses)
- Domain: job interview training / human resources
- Country / region: not reported
- Recruitment method: convenience (computer science course participants)

## Transparency operationalization
- Type: disclosure / up-front model description (pre-interaction framing)
- Manipulation or measure: Between-subjects manipulation — participants in the "advanced" condition were told the system was powered by Meta AI's RoBERTa deep neural network trained on 63 million articles; the "basic" condition described a graduate student's NLTK decision tree trained on 150 transcripts. All participants received identical system outputs (Wizard-of-Oz).
- Scale / instrument name: not applicable (binary between-subjects manipulation)
- Number of items: not applicable

## Trust operationalization
- Type: cognitive (self-report) and behavioural (reliance/alignment)
- Measure: (1) Self-reported trust via adapted Körber Trust in Automation scale and Madsen & Gregor Human-Computer Trust items; (2) Behavioural trust via human-AI rating agreement (Mann-Whitney U comparison of agreement with system numerical ratings), pre-to-post rating shift toward the AI, willingness to apply feedback, and willingness to use the system in future.
- Scale / instrument name: Körber Trust in Automation scale; Madsen & Gregor Human-Computer Trust scale
- Number of items: not reported
- Behavioural vs self-report: both used

## Moderators and covariates tested
- Interviewing confidence: measured as individual difference covariate; no significant moderation found
- Big-5 personality traits: measured as individual difference covariates; no significant moderation found
- General propensity to trust AI: measured as individual difference covariate; no significant moderation found
- User technical background (computer science students): implicit sample characteristic; authors note framing effect may require minimum technical literacy to process DNN vs. decision tree descriptions (relevant to RQ3)

## Statistical approach
- Primary analysis method: Mann-Whitney U tests (non-parametric between-group comparisons for each dependent variable)
- Software: not reported
- Key model fit or effect size reported: effect size r reported for each comparison (r = .31–.44 for significant findings); p-values reported

## Author-noted limitations
- Small sample (N = 42) limits statistical power and generalisability
- All participants had technical background (CS students); results may not generalise to non-technical users
- Single-session experiment; no longitudinal follow-up to assess persistence of framing effects
- Wizard-of-Oz design means participants received identical feedback regardless of condition; real-world differences in model quality may produce different effects
- Only one domain (job interview training) tested; generalisability across contexts unknown
- No manipulation check confirming participants processed and retained the model description
