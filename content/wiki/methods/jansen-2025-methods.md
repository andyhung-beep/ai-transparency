# Jansen et al. 2025 — Methods

**Summary**: A three-day longitudinal within-subjects online study (N=50) examining how repeated exposure to low-abstraction uncertainty visualizations of automated vehicle perception functions (Situation Detection and Situation Prediction) affects user trust, perceived safety, situation awareness, and mental workload over time.

**Sources**: Jansen et al._Longitudinal effects of visualizing uncertainty of situation detection.pdf

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1, RQ2, RQ3

**Last updated**: 2026-04-19

---

## Study design
quasi-experimental (longitudinal within-subjects repeated-measures design; no randomization to transparency vs. no-transparency conditions — all participants received visualizations)

## Sample
- N: 50
- Population: general public (US-based adults; sample skewed younger)
- Domain: automated vehicles / transportation
- Country / region: United States
- Recruitment method: not reported (online study; Prolific or similar platform implied but not confirmed)

## Transparency operationalization
- Type: uncertainty visualization / process transparency — low-abstraction visual overlays on driving video showing the AV's real-time neural network outputs: semantic segmentation for Situation Detection (vehicles in blue, vulnerable road users in red, traffic signs in yellow) and pedestrian intention icons for Situation Prediction
- Manipulation or measure: within-subjects longitudinal exposure — all participants viewed real-world urban driving videos overlaid with uncertainty visualizations twice daily (morning and evening sessions) over three consecutive days (six sessions total); videos generated by state-of-the-art neural networks (InternImage mIoU=86.1%; pedestrian crossing AP=83.1%), faithfully conveying model imperfections (misclassifications, false positives, flickering)
- Scale / instrument name: not applicable (naturalistic visualization exposure, not a scale)
- Number of items: not applicable

## Trust operationalization
- Type: cognitive trust (trust in automation), perceived safety, predictability / understandability
- Measure: Körber (2019) Trust in Automation questionnaire — Trust in Automation subscale (two 5-point Likert items) and Predictability/Understandability subscale (four 5-point Likert items); perceived safety via Faas et al. (2020) semantic differentials; acceptance via Van Der Laan et al. (1997) usefulness/satisfaction scales
- Scale / instrument name: Trust in Automation questionnaire (Körber, 2019); Faas et al. (2020) perceived safety scale; Van Der Laan et al. (1997) acceptance scale
- Number of items: Trust in Automation subscale 2 items; Predictability/Understandability subscale 4 items; others not specified
- Behavioural vs self-report: self-report (all measures are Likert / semantic differential ratings)

## Moderators and covariates tested
- Time of day (morning vs. evening session): measured as within-subjects factor; evening sessions consistently rated higher than morning sessions on trust, driving style safety, and clarity — a contextual moderator of the transparency–trust trajectory
- Day of study (Day 1 / 2 / 3): primary longitudinal factor; significant increases in trust and perceived safety by Day 3 vs. Day 1
- Visualization type (Situation Detection vs. Situation Prediction): not formally compared statistically but qualitative feedback distinguished them; pedestrian intention icons criticized more than vehicle detection overlays
- User expertise / AI literacy: not measured or manipulated; sample is general-public lay users only — relevant to RQ3 in that all findings apply to non-expert users

## Statistical approach
- Primary analysis method: repeated-measures ANOVA or equivalent within-subjects comparison across sessions and days; pairwise comparisons with adjustment for multiple comparisons (adj. p reported)
- Software: not reported
- Key model fit or effect size reported: trust M=3.43 (Day 1) vs. M=3.92 (Day 3), adj. p<0.001; perceived safety M=0.94 (Day 1) vs. M=1.60 (Day 3), adj. p=0.002; predictability M=3.83 (Day 1) vs. M=4.05 (Day 3), adj. p=0.040; effect sizes not reported

## Author-noted limitations
- Absolute magnitude of improvements was small despite statistical significance (e.g., ~0.5 points on a 5-point trust scale over three days)
- Sample skewed younger; generalisability to older or less tech-savvy users is uncertain
- No control (no-visualization) condition included in the longitudinal design; pre–post comparison is within one transparency condition, not a transparency vs. no-transparency comparison
- Study conducted online with pre-recorded videos, not in a physical or simulated AV environment
- Incorrect or flickering visualizations were observed to slow or temporarily reverse trust growth, but this was not formally modeled as a moderator
- Situation awareness (SART) did not change significantly, despite subjective gaps in understanding AV actions reported by participants
