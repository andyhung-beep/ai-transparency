# Yeganejou 2020 — Methods

**Summary**: A technical paper presenting a hybrid deep convolutional fuzzy classifier architecture evaluated on three benchmark computer-vision datasets (MNIST, Fashion MNIST, CIFAR-10), proposing cluster medoid saliency maps as an interpretability mechanism without a formal user study.

## Transparency operationalization
- Type: interpretability-by-design (intrinsic interpretability via fuzzy logic integration; supplemented by post-hoc saliency maps)
- Manipulation or measure: No experimental manipulation. The paper proposes and evaluates an architectural approach: (1) A CNN serves as an automated feature extractor; (2) The last convolutional layer's output is passed to a fuzzy clustering algorithm (FCM or Gustafson–Kessel); (3) Rocchio's algorithm performs classification; (4) Explanations are generated by identifying the medoid of each fuzzy cluster and applying guided backpropagation, Taylor decomposition, and layerwise relevance propagation (LRP) to produce pixel-level saliency maps showing which image regions are most relevant to each predicted class. Accuracy compared across architectures (LeNet variants, AlexNet, ResNets) and clustering methods (FCM vs. GK).

- Number of items: not applicable

## Trust operationalization

The paper does not conduct a user study and explicitly calls for future user studies to assess whether saliency map explanations are comprehensible to non-expert populations.

## Statistical approach
- Primary analysis method: classification accuracy comparison across architectures, clustering algorithms (FCM vs. GK), and datasets (MNIST, Fashion MNIST, CIFAR-10); accuracy reported as percentage on held-out test sets
- Software: not reported
- Key model fit or effect size reported: classification accuracy percentages per architecture/clustering combination; accuracy gap between deep fuzzy classifier and base CNN (typically <1.5 percentage points); GK consistently outperformed FCM across datasets

## Author-noted limitations
- No formal user study conducted; interpretability claimed but not empirically validated with human users
- Accuracy consistently lower than base CNN (though gap is modest); accuracy–interpretability trade-off acknowledged
- Evaluation limited to three benchmark datasets; generalisability to other image domains or non-visual tasks unknown
- Fuzzy membership functions and cluster shapes may not align with human-interpretable concepts in all domains
- Authors explicitly note the need for user studies to test whether explanations are comprehensible to non-expert populations
