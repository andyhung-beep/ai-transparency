# Spitzer et al. (2025) — Methods

**Summary**: A mixed within-subjects (explanation correctness) and between-subjects (explanation modality) experiment with 136 participants of varying birding expertise examining how imperfect XAI explanations, user expertise, and cognitive style jointly affect appropriate reliance and human–AI team performance in a bird species identification task.

**Sources**: Spitzer et al._Imperfections of XAI.pdf

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1 / RQ2 / RQ3

**Last updated**: 2026-04-19

---

## Study design
experimental (mixed within-subjects × between-subjects design)

## Sample
- N: 136
- Population: general public with varying birding expertise (self-reported); screened to include novices and experienced birdwatchers
- Domain: wildlife conservation / citizen science (bird species identification)
- Country / region: not reported
- Recruitment method: not reported

## Transparency operationalization
- Type: post-hoc explanation — natural language explanations vs. example-based (visual) explanations; additionally manipulated for explanation correctness (correct vs. incorrect fidelity to the AI's predicted class)
- Manipulation or measure: between-subjects manipulation of explanation modality (natural language description generated by a language model vs. three most visually similar training images retrieved via cosine similarity); within-subjects manipulation of explanation correctness (aligned vs. misaligned with AI predicted class)
- Scale / instrument name: not applicable
- Number of items: not applicable

## Trust operationalization
- Type: behavioural (reliance-based; no self-report trust scale administered)
- Measure: appropriate reliance operationalized via Correct AI Reliance Rate (RAIR) — proportion of trials where participant correctly follows AI advice when AI is correct; Correct Self-Reliance Rate (RSR) — proportion of trials where participant correctly overrides AI when AI is wrong; Deception of Reliance (DoR) — novel metric quantifying extent to which incorrect explanations mislead decision-making; Complementary Team Performance (CTP) — whether human–AI dyad outperforms both human and AI alone
- Scale / instrument name: not applicable (behavioural metrics derived from decision logs, following Schemmer et al., 2023)
- Number of items: not applicable
- Behavioural vs self-report: behavioural

## Moderators and covariates tested
- User expertise (birding domain): self-reported level of birding experience; tested as between-subjects moderator of explanation correctness effects on RAIR and RSR — relevant to RQ3
- Cognitive style (verbal vs. visual): assessed as a continuous individual-difference measure; tested as moderator of explanation modality effects on RSR and team performance — relevant to RQ3
- AI advice accuracy: within-subjects (correct vs. incorrect AI prediction) crossed with explanation correctness

## Statistical approach
- Primary analysis method: mixed-model ANOVA; linear regression for cognitive style effects; signal detection or proportion-based analyses for RAIR, RSR, DoR, and CTP metrics
- Software: not reported
- Key model fit or effect size reported: interaction effect sizes and means reported (e.g., expert RSR correct = 0.29 vs. incorrect = 0.57; DoR for natural language experts = 0.11 vs. example-based experts = 0.32); p-values reported for key interactions

## Author-noted limitations
- Sample recruited without strict random sampling; birding expertise distribution may not represent the general citizen science population
- The bird species identification task is domain-specific; findings may not generalise to other XAI application domains
- Self-report cognitive style measures may not fully capture actual information processing strategies
- Natural language explanations were generated by a language model, introducing variability in explanation quality beyond the correctness manipulation
- The study does not include self-report trust measures, limiting comparison with the broader XAI–trust literature that relies on Likert-scale instruments
