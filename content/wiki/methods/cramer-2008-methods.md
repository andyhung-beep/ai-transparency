# Cramer 2008 — Methods

**Summary**: A between-subjects experiment (N = 60) with a content-based art recommender system comparing three transparency conditions — no transparency, "why" explanations, and confidence ratings — finding that "why" explanations increased recommendation acceptance and actual understanding but not trust in the system.

**Sources**: Cramer et al._The_effects_of_transparency_on.pdf

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1 / RQ2 / RQ3

**Last updated**: 2026-04-19

---

## Study design
experimental (between-subjects, with qualitative think-aloud and structured interview supplement; 8-week follow-up questionnaire)

## Sample
- N: 60
- Population: general public (museum/art interest not specified as prerequisite; one art expert participant noted in qualitative analysis)
- Domain: cultural heritage / e-culture / recommender systems
- Country / region: Netherlands (Rijksmuseum Amsterdam collection used)
- Recruitment method: not reported

## Transparency operationalization
- Type: process transparency ("why" explanations) and uncertainty visualization (confidence ratings)
- Manipulation or measure: Three between-subjects conditions: (1) Non-transparent (Non) — recommendations with no explanation; (2) Transparent/Why — a "why?" link per recommendation opening a pop-up listing shared artwork properties that triggered the recommendation (local content-based rationale); (3) Confidence rating (Sure) — a percentage below each recommendation indicating the system's estimated confidence that the user would like the artwork.
- Scale / instrument name: not applicable (between-subjects manipulation)
- Number of items: not applicable

## Trust operationalization
- Type: cognitive (self-report) and behavioural (recommendation acceptance and system adoption)
- Measure: (1) Cognitive trust questionnaire adapted from Jian et al. (2000), 10 items (e.g., "I trust the system", "I can depend on the system", "The system is reliable"), α = .901; (2) Behavioural trust — number of recommended artworks included in the participant's final selection of six artworks; (3) System adoption — acceptance scenario question measuring willingness to delegate artwork selection to the recommender under time pressure vs. browsing a catalogue manually; (4) Intent to use — 3-item scale, α = .914.
- Scale / instrument name: Jian et al. (2000) trust scale (adapted)
- Number of items: 10 (trust); 3 (intent to use)
- Behavioural vs self-report: both used

## Moderators and covariates tested
- Art domain expertise: noted qualitatively; one art expert was more critical of "why" explanations due to oversimplification — not formally tested as moderator but flagged as relevant to RQ3
- Technical knowledge (recommender system familiarity): noted as potential moderator in discussion; not formally measured
- Perceived understanding vs. actual understanding: both measured; correlation between them was non-significant (Spearman's rho = .005), revealing a metacognitive gap

## Statistical approach
- Primary analysis method: Mann-Whitney U tests (non-parametric between-group comparisons); Spearman's rank correlations for relationships between perceived understanding, actual understanding, trust, and intent to use; qualitative think-aloud and interview coding (independent raters)
- Software: not reported
- Key model fit or effect size reported: p-values (one-tailed) reported for Mann-Whitney comparisons; Spearman's rho reported for correlations (e.g., rho = .450 for perceived understanding and trust; rho = .805 for trust and intent to use); α = .901 (trust scale), α = .914 (intent to use scale)

## Author-noted limitations
- Small sample (N = 60) limits statistical power
- Single domain (art recommender / cultural heritage); generalisability to other recommendation domains unknown
- Confidence ratings poorly understood by participants; construct validity of this manipulation may be limited
- No control for prior art knowledge or familiarity with recommender systems
- Think-aloud may influence naturalistic behaviour
- Long-term follow-up (8 weeks) had reduced response rate; longitudinal analysis limited
