# Schmager 2024 — Methods

**Summary**: A qualitative Action Design Research study with 20 Norwegian citizens who interacted with a prototype AI-assisted sick-leave prediction tool and were interviewed to explore their stances on AI use in public welfare services through a Social Contract Theory lens.

**Sources**: Schmager et al._exploring-citizens-stances-on-ai-in-public-services-a-social-contract-perspective-div.pdf

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1 / RQ2 / RQ3

**Last updated**: 2026-04-19

---

## Study design
qualitative (Action Design Research with semi-structured interviews; prototype-based)

## Sample
- N: 20
- Population: general public (Norwegian citizens)
- Domain: public services / welfare (sick-leave duration prediction)
- Country / region: Norway
- Recruitment method: not reported (convenience)

## Transparency operationalization
- Type: process transparency, outcome transparency, and disclosure (multi-level)
- Manipulation or measure: Participants interacted with an interactive prototype of an AI-assisted sick-leave duration prediction tool. Transparency features embedded in the prototype included: (a) a process chart showing how the AI is used in the workflow; (b) a data table displaying what data are collected and for what purpose; (c) natural language explanations of the AI's prediction basis. The study did not experimentally manipulate transparency levels — all participants received the full prototype. A 5-point comfort-with-AI rating was collected before and after prototype interaction. Qualitative interview data analysed thematically using Social Contract Theory (SCT) at three levels: macro (legitimacy and power), meso (individual vs. collective balance), micro (interaction processes).
- Scale / instrument name: not applicable (qualitative design; comfort rating = 5-point scale)
- Number of items: not applicable

## Trust operationalization
- Type: institutional trust (macro), cognitive trust (micro), and behavioural (willingness to consent)
- Measure: (1) 5-point comfort-with-AI rating collected before and after prototype interaction; (2) Qualitative interview responses coded thematically for trust-related stances at macro, meso, and micro levels using SCT. Institutional trust in the Norwegian government was assessed qualitatively as a contextual antecedent. Willingness to consent to AI use without reading full disclosure used as a behavioural trust indicator.
- Scale / instrument name: not applicable (custom 5-point comfort rating; qualitative coding)
- Number of items: 1 (comfort rating)
- Behavioural vs self-report: both (comfort rating = self-report; consent behaviour = behavioural)

## Moderators and covariates tested
- Pre-existing institutional trust in government: qualitative moderator — citizens with high government trust accepted AI without detailed scrutiny; relevant to RQ2 as a boundary condition
- Statistical and bureaucratic literacy: qualitative finding — lower literacy created barriers to understanding AI explanations; relevant to RQ3
- Note: no formal statistical moderation testing conducted (qualitative design)

## Statistical approach
- Primary analysis method: thematic analysis (qualitative coding using Social Contract Theory framework); descriptive statistics for comfort rating changes
- Software: not reported
- Key model fit or effect size reported: not applicable (qualitative study); descriptive proportions reported (40% increased comfort, ~40% unchanged, 20% decreased)

## Author-noted limitations
- Small qualitative sample (N = 20); findings are not statistically generalisable
- Single country (Norway) with high baseline institutional trust in government; may not generalise to lower-trust contexts
- Prototype rather than a deployed system — participants may respond differently to real AI systems
- No expert participants; findings apply to general public only
- Qualitative coding introduces subjectivity; inter-rater reliability not reported
