# Lim et al. 2025 — Methods

**Summary**: A 3 × 2 between-subjects online experiment (N = 464) testing how AI versus human authorship attribution interacts with relational tone (warmth vs. competence) to influence trust and forgiveness in a simulated corporate data-breach crisis apology.

**Sources**: Lim et al._How warm-versus competent-toned AI apologies affect trust and forgiveness.pdf

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1, RQ2

**Last updated**: 2026-04-19

---

## Study design
experimental

## Sample
- N: 464
- Population: general adult public (US)
- Domain: organisational crisis communication / corporate PR
- Country / region: United States
- Recruitment method: Prolific Academic

## Transparency operationalization
- Type: disclosure — source attribution of AI-generated content
- Manipulation or measure: participants were shown a simulated X (formerly Twitter) corporate apology post from a fictitious brand (Novera) with authorship indicated by a detection-tool-style interface displaying either "100% AI" or "100% Human" (or a no-attribution control); a 3 (authorship: AI / Human / control) × 2 (tone: warmth / competence) factorial design was used
- Scale / instrument name: not applicable (experimental manipulation)
- Number of items: not applicable

## Trust operationalization
- Type: cognitive and affective (multi-dimensional: integrity, dependability, competence sub-dimensions)
- Measure: 8-item scale adapted from Lim and Jiang (2021) covering integrity, dependability, and competence; M = 4.06, SD = 1.25, α = .96
- Scale / instrument name: Lim and Jiang (2021) trust scale (adapted)
- Number of items: 8
- Behavioural vs self-report: self-report; forgiveness intention (5-item scale, α = .93) used as a downstream behavioural trust proxy

## Moderators and covariates tested
- Relational tone (warmth vs. competence): manipulated between-subjects; tone moderated the effect of authorship attribution on negative emotions and perceived sincerity — relevant to RQ2
- Authorship attribution (AI / Human / control): primary independent variable; not a moderator per se but the key transparency-related manipulation
- User expertise / AI literacy: not examined; sample is general public, mean age 46.2

## Statistical approach
- Primary analysis method: ANOVA for main effects and interaction effects; PROCESS macro (Hayes, 2018) for moderated mediation
- Software: not reported
- Key model fit or effect size reported: interaction F(1,302) = 4.40, p = 0.037 (warmth × AI authorship on negative emotions); moderated mediation β = 0.20, p < 0.05, BCa 95% CI [0.01, 0.38]; Cronbach's α = .96 (trust), α = .93 (forgiveness intention)

## Author-noted limitations
- User expertise and AI familiarity not measured or controlled for; effects may vary with experience
- Single crisis type (data breach) and single brand limit generalisability to other crisis domains
- Prolific sample may not represent all consumer demographics
- Study relies on a simulated social media post; ecological validity of lab-based crisis scenarios is inherently limited
- Cultural variation in perceptions of AI authorship not addressed
