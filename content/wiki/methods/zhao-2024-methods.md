# Zhao 2024 — Methods

**Summary**: Four pre-registered between-subjects experiments (total N = 1,211) in Chinese workplace HR contexts demonstrating that perceived opacity mediates algorithm aversion, and that providing transparency information or anthropomorphising algorithms can significantly mitigate this aversion.

**Sources**: Zhao et al._Perceived opacity leads to algorithm aversion in the workplace.pdf

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1 / RQ2 / RQ3

**Last updated**: 2026-04-19

---

## Study design
experimental (four pre-registered between-subjects experiments; online)

## Sample
- N: 1,211 total (Experiment 1: N = 303; Experiment 2: N = 179; Experiment 3: N = 180; Experiment 4: N = 549)
- Population: general public / working adults (Chinese online platform participants; employee and employer perspectives sampled)
- Domain: HR / workplace (resume screening, recruitment/hiring, bonus allocation, performance assessment)
- Country / region: China (Wuhan University; platforms: Credamo, PowerCX)
- Recruitment method: online crowdsourcing platforms (Credamo, PowerCX)

## Transparency operationalization
- Type: algorithmic transparency / disclosure (perceived transparency of decision process)
- Manipulation or measure: Varied across experiments: (1) Experiment 1 — no transparency manipulation; algorithm vs. human decision-maker compared; perceived transparency measured as a mediator; (2) Experiment 2 — replication of Experiment 1 with explicit measure of perceived transparency to test mediation; (3) Experiment 3 — direct manipulation of transparency: high-transparency condition provided additional information and decision principles about the algorithm; low-transparency condition provided no such information; (4) Experiment 4 — three-level condition: non-anthropomorphic algorithm vs. anthropomorphic algorithm (human-like name and communication style) vs. human decision-maker; tests anthropomorphism as an alternative route to aversion mitigation. HR scenarios: resume screening (Exp. 1), recruitment/hiring (Exp. 2, 3), bonus allocation (Exp. 3), performance assessment (Exp. 4).
- Scale / instrument name: perceived transparency measured via Likert items (not reported by name)
- Number of items: not reported

## Trust operationalization
- Type: tripartite — cognitive (permissibility), affective (liking), and behavioural (willingness to utilise)
- Measure: Algorithm aversion operationalised as three dimensions: (1) Permissibility — cognitive acceptability rating (rational/normative judgment of whether algorithmic decision-making is acceptable); (2) Liking — affective preference rating; (3) Willingness to utilise — behavioural intention to use algorithmic decision-making. All measured via Likert-scale self-report items. Comparisons made between algorithm vs. human decision-maker conditions across all four experiments.
- Scale / instrument name: not reported (Likert items for each of the three aversion dimensions)
- Number of items: not reported
- Behavioural vs self-report: self-report (willingness to utilise as behavioural intention proxy; no actual behavioural measure)

## Moderators and covariates tested
- Anthropomorphism: tested as moderating boundary condition in Experiment 4 (anthropomorphic vs. non-anthropomorphic algorithm vs. human); anthropomorphism modestly reduced aversion (F(2, 546) = 3.15, p = 0.044, η²p = 0.011)
- Familiarity and prior experience with algorithms: discussed as potential moderator in discussion section but not systematically tested
- Employee vs. employer perspective: noted as dual-perspective design element; both perspectives examined across scenarios but not formally tested as a moderator

## Statistical approach
- Primary analysis method: t-tests and one-way ANOVA for between-group mean comparisons (permissibility, liking, willingness to utilise); mediation analysis using bootstrapping (PROCESS macro) to test perceived transparency as mediator of algorithm aversion (Experiments 2 and 3); effect sizes (Cohen's d) reported for Experiment 3 manipulations
- Software: PROCESS macro (Hayes); broader software not reported
- Key model fit or effect size reported: Experiment 2 mediation: indirect effect on permissibility −0.50 (95% CI [−0.96, −0.08]); indirect effect on combined aversion −0.25 (95% CI [−0.51, −0.04]); Experiment 3 Cohen's d ≈ 0.54–0.62 for transparency manipulation effects; Experiment 4 η²p = 0.011 for anthropomorphism

## Author-noted limitations
- Vignette/scenario-based design; results may not fully generalise to real workplace algorithm deployment
- All studies conducted in China; cultural factors in algorithm attitudes may limit cross-cultural generalisation
- Anthropomorphism effect (Experiment 4) is modest (η²p = 0.011); practical significance of anthropomorphism as an aversion mitigation strategy is limited
- User expertise and algorithm familiarity not experimentally varied; role of prior experience not fully tested
- Self-report willingness to utilise is a behavioural intention proxy, not actual behaviour
- Algorithm type and quality held constant; effects of algorithm accuracy on aversion not examined
