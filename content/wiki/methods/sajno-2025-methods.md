# Sajno 2025 — Methods

**Summary**: A pre-registered study protocol (no empirical results yet reported) for a planned between-subjects online experiment (target N ≈ 550) testing the effects of Learning to Defer (LtD), LtD + XAI, and hidden AI delegation on decision accuracy, cognitive processing style, and trust in a noisy image classification task.

**Sources**: Sajno et al._AI Says I'm Better.pdf

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1 / RQ2 / RQ3

**Last updated**: 2026-04-19

---

## Study design
study protocol (pre-registered experimental design; no empirical findings reported)

## Sample
- N: approximately 550 (planned; not yet collected)
- Population: general public (online participants)
- Domain: general cognitive task (noisy image classification; intended clinical decision-making application)
- Country / region: not reported
- Recruitment method: online (platform not specified)

## Transparency operationalization
- Type: process transparency (Learning to Defer — AI explicitly delegates decisions to user) and explainability (GradCAM visual saliency explanation)
- Manipulation or measure: Three between-subjects conditions: (1) LtD (Defer) — AI explicitly signals uncertainty and delegates decision authority to the participant; (2) LtD + XAI (Defer + Explanation) — AI defers and provides a GradCAM visual explanation of its image processing; (3) Hidden Delegation — AI has processed the image but does not disclose its involvement; participant is unaware of AI. Crossed with two stakes conditions: neutral narrative vs. high-stakes narrative (dangerous object framing).
- Scale / instrument name: not applicable (experimental manipulation)
- Number of items: not applicable

## Trust operationalization
- Type: behavioural (accuracy, response time, agreement with AI) and dispositional (AI attitude scales)
- Measure: (1) Decision accuracy (objective performance); (2) Response time as a proxy for System 1 vs. System 2 processing; (3) Agreement with AI classification; (4) General Attitudes towards AI Scale (GAAIS) as a dispositional trust/attitude measure; (5) Intolerance of Uncertainty Scale (IUS-12). No dedicated self-reported trust scale specified in the protocol.
- Scale / instrument name: General Attitudes towards AI Scale (GAAIS); Intolerance of Uncertainty Scale (IUS-12)
- Number of items: not reported
- Behavioural vs self-report: primarily behavioural (planned); dispositional attitude scales used as moderators

## Moderators and covariates tested
- Analytic vs. intuitive decision-making style (Cognitive Reflection Test or similar): planned moderator — expected to increase engagement with LtD + XAI; relevant to RQ3
- AI attitudes (GAAIS): planned moderator — expected to moderate trust and engagement with transparency conditions; relevant to RQ3
- Intolerance of uncertainty (IUS-12): planned moderator — expected to reduce trust when explanations are absent; relevant to RQ3
- Emotion regulation strategy (suppressive vs. expressive): planned moderator; relevant to RQ3
- Stakes level (neutral vs. high-stakes framing): planned between-subjects moderator

## Statistical approach
- Primary analysis method: ANOVA / mixed ANOVA (planned; conditions × stakes × individual difference moderators)
- Software: not reported
- Key model fit or effect size reported: not reported (study protocol — no results)

## Author-noted limitations
- No empirical results available; all hypotheses remain to be confirmed
- Online convenience sample may not represent clinical professionals (the intended application domain)
- GradCAM explanation quality and participant comprehension not pre-validated
- Noisy image classification may not capture the complexity of real clinical decisions
- Hidden delegation condition ethical implications (deception) noted but not elaborated
