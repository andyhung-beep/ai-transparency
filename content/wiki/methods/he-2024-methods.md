# He et al. 2024 — Methods

**Summary**: A two-phase study comprising a crowd-based analogy generation and expert evaluation study followed by a between-subjects human-AI decision-making experiment (N=280) evaluating whether analogy-based explanations improve appropriate reliance in a skin cancer detection task.

**Sources**: He et al._Opening the analogical portal to explainability.pdf

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1, RQ2, RQ3

**Last updated**: 2026-04-19

---

## Study design
mixed-methods (crowd-computing study for analogy generation + experimental between-subjects study for human-AI decision making)

## Sample
- N: 280 (human-AI decision-making study); 600 analogies generated via crowd-computing (separate phase); expert evaluation involved domain experts for analogy quality rating
- Population: general public / crowdworkers (decision-making study); domain experts (analogy quality evaluation)
- Domain: healthcare (skin cancer detection / medical imaging)
- Country / region: not reported
- Recruitment method: not reported (crowdsourcing platform implied for both phases; specific platform not named)

## Transparency operationalization
- Type: explainability — concept-level XAI outputs from a concept bottleneck model, enhanced with analogy-based natural-language explanations
- Manipulation or measure: between-subjects manipulation with multiple conditions: (1) no explanation (control); (2) concept importance only (Concept-Imp — target domain information); (3) analogy conditions (full analogy-based explanations mapping unfamiliar medical concepts to familiar everyday domains); crowd-computing phase produced 600 analogies rated on nine quality dimensions (structural correspondence, relational similarity, transferability, helpfulness, familiarity, simplicity, misunderstanding, syntactic correctness, factual correctness)
- Scale / instrument name: analogy quality evaluated on nine researcher-defined dimensions; not a published validated scale
- Number of items: nine dimensions for analogy quality rating; not applicable for XAI manipulation

## Trust operationalization
- Type: behavioural trust (appropriate reliance) as primary outcome; cognitive trust measured via post-task concept identification
- Measure: behavioural — Switch Fraction (proportion of AI recommendations accepted), Relative Positive AI Reliance (RAIR; appropriate agreement with correct AI), Relative Positive Self-Reliance (RSR; appropriate override of incorrect AI); self-report — Trust in Automation (TiA) questionnaire covering reliability/competence, understanding/predictability, propensity to trust, overall trust in automation
- Scale / instrument name: Trust in Automation (TiA) questionnaire (Körber, 2018)
- Number of items: not reported (TiA subscale items not specified in source page)
- Behavioural vs self-report: both (primary outcomes are behavioural reliance measures; TiA is self-report)

## Moderators and covariates tested
- User medical expertise / domain knowledge: measured as a covariate; skin cancer knowledge and general medical domain expertise measured; found that understanding of commonsense explanations varied with recipient experience — directly relevant to RQ3
- Cognitive load: measured via NASA-TLX; no significant effect of analogy conditions on cognitive load
- Decision-making efficiency: measured as time-on-task; no significant delay from analogy conditions
- Analogy quality dimensions: structural correspondence, relational similarity, familiarity, simplicity, transferability correlated with perceived helpfulness in the crowd phase

## Statistical approach
- Primary analysis method: ANOVA / statistical comparison of reliance metrics across conditions (exact method not fully specified in source page); correlation analysis for analogy quality dimensions and helpfulness
- Software: not reported
- Key model fit or effect size reported: not reported (statistical significance thresholds for between-condition differences not fully specified; null result for full analogy vs. concept-only on RAIR/RSR reported)

## Author-noted limitations
- Analogy quality as generated by the crowd was high on simplicity and familiarity but low on structural correspondence and relational similarity, suggesting current crowd methods do not reliably produce structurally accurate analogies
- Full analogy conditions did not significantly improve appropriate reliance, limiting practical utility of the approach as implemented
- Human intuition and perceived plausibility sometimes overrode informational content of analogies, suggesting non-rational reliance mechanisms that analogies cannot fully address
- Generalisation beyond skin cancer detection task is uncertain
- Sample characteristics and recruitment method not fully reported, limiting replication
