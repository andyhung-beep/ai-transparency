# Du 2019 — Methods

**Summary**: A within-subjects driving simulator experiment (N = 32) examining how the timing of automated vehicle verbal explanations (before-action, after-action, permission-required, or no explanation) affects driver trust, preference, anxiety, and mental workload.

**Sources**: Du et al._Look who is talking now.pdf

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1 / RQ2 / RQ3

**Last updated**: 2026-04-19

---

## Study design
experimental (within-subjects, counterbalanced via Latin square; high-fidelity driving simulator)

## Sample
- N: 32
- Population: general public / young adults (mean age = 26.9 years)
- Domain: autonomous vehicles / transportation
- Country / region: United States (University of Michigan Transportation Research Institute)
- Recruitment method: not reported (university-affiliated convenience sample implied)

## Transparency operationalization
- Type: outcome transparency / natural language explanations — real-time verbal explanations of AV decisions
- Manipulation or measure: Within-subjects manipulation — four explanation timing conditions presented to each participant (counterbalanced): (1) No explanation (NExpl); (2) Before-action explanation (BExpl) — verbal explanation delivered 7 seconds prior to the AV action; (3) After-action explanation (AExpl) — verbal explanation delivered within 1 second after the AV action; (4) Permission-required (PermReq) — before-action explanation with driver permission required to proceed. Each condition contained three unexpected events (oversized vehicle, road reroute, approaching police vehicle). Explanations communicated the reason for the AV action in natural language (e.g., "Oversized vehicle blocking roadway. Slowing down.").
- Scale / instrument name: not applicable (between-condition timing manipulation)
- Number of items: not applicable

## Trust operationalization
- Type: cognitive (self-report, multidimensional scale) and behavioural (ranking/preference)
- Measure: (1) Muir (1987) trust-in-automation scale — 6 items covering competence, predictability, dependability, responsibility, reliability, and faith on a 7-point scale (α = .923); (2) Trust ranking — ordinal ranking of the four conditions by participants after all conditions were completed; (3) Preference for AV — 8-item scale (α = .885), partially capturing affective trust/preference.
- Scale / instrument name: Muir (1987) trust-in-automation scale
- Number of items: 6 (trust scale); 8 (AV preference scale)
- Behavioural vs self-report: self-report (trust scale and ranking); AV preference self-report

## Moderators and covariates tested
- Desirability of control: noted as potential individual difference moderator in limitations; not formally measured or analysed
- Personality traits: noted as potential moderator in limitations; not formally measured
- Age: sample mean reported (M = 26.9 years) but not analysed as moderator — relevant to RQ3 only as a sample characteristic
- Note: no explicit user expertise or AI literacy variable examined; RQ3 relevance is indirect

## Statistical approach
- Primary analysis method: repeated-measures ANOVA (F(3,93) = 4.814 for trust across conditions); post-hoc pairwise comparisons for specific condition differences; Friedman test for ordinal trust rankings
- Software: not reported
- Key model fit or effect size reported: F(3,93) = 4.814, p = .008 (trust ANOVA); specific post-hoc p-values reported (e.g., BExpl vs. NExpl p = .013; BExpl vs. AExpl p = .018; PermReq ranked higher than NExpl and AExpl p < .001); α = .923 (trust scale), α = .885 (AV preference scale)

## Author-noted limitations
- Small sample (N = 32) limits statistical power and moderator analyses
- Simulator environment may not capture real-world trust dynamics (ecological validity concern)
- Only young adults sampled; older or more experienced drivers may respond differently to AV explanations
- Individual differences in desirability of control and personality not measured; may moderate explanation timing effects
- Within-subjects design risks carryover effects despite Latin square counterbalancing
- Only unexpected events tested; explanations for routine AV decisions not examined
