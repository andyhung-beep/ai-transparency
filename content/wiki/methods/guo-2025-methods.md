# Guo & Chen 2025 — Methods

**Summary**: A 3×2×2 between-subjects randomized factorial online experiment (N=249) examining how consultation source (autonomous AI vs. human-involved AI vs. human physician), health-related stigma, and provision of a diagnosis explanation affect patients' functional, relational, and emotional evaluations of medical consultations.

**Sources**: Guo & Chen_Investigating whether AI will replace human physicians.pdf

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1, RQ2, RQ3

**Last updated**: 2026-04-19

---

## Study design
experimental

## Sample
- N: 249
- Population: undergraduate students (mean age 18.67)
- Domain: healthcare (medical consultation / AI physician)
- Country / region: not reported (implied China or US based on publication venue; journal is JMIR)
- Recruitment method: convenience (undergraduate student participants)

## Transparency operationalization
- Type: outcome transparency and process transparency combined; delivered as natural-language explanation in a simulated chat interface
- Manipulation or measure: between-subjects manipulation — explanation condition received a multi-component diagnosis explanation covering symptom analysis, possible diagnoses, rationale for specific diagnosis, treatment options with rationale, and medication efficacy; no-explanation condition received diagnosis and treatment options only
- Scale / instrument name: not applicable (manipulated via scenario text)
- Number of items: not applicable

## Trust operationalization
- Type: cognitive trust (source credibility, message credibility), behavioural trust (persuasive effect / treatment adherence intentions), and affective trust (perceived empathy, self-disclosure comfort, psychological distance)
- Measure: multi-item Likert scales for each sub-dimension; source credibility (trustworthiness, competence, knowledge, goodwill); message credibility (accuracy, authenticity, believability); persuasive effect (5 items including willingness to adhere, willingness to consult again); perceived empathy (5 items); self-disclosure; psychological distance
- Scale / instrument name: not reported (adapted scales)
- Number of items: source credibility 4 items; message credibility 3 items; persuasive effect 5 items; perceived empathy 5 items; self-disclosure and psychological distance not reported
- Behavioural vs self-report: self-report (all measures are self-reported Likert items)

## Moderators and covariates tested
- Consultation source (autonomous AI vs. human-involved AI vs. human physician) — between-subjects factor; primary independent variable
- Health-related stigma (AIDS — high stigma vs. heart disease — low stigma) — between-subjects factor; tested as potential moderator of source preference; not user expertise but condition characteristic
- Interaction between stigma and explanation: found that stigmatized-condition participants rated source credibility higher under no-explanation condition, effect disappears with explanation
- Interaction between consultation source and explanation on perceived empathy: with explanation, human physicians elicit more empathy than AI agents; without explanation, no difference
- User expertise / AI literacy: not measured or manipulated (not relevant to RQ3)

## Statistical approach
- Primary analysis method: factorial ANOVA / MANOVA (3×2×2 design); interaction effects tested
- Software: not reported
- Key model fit or effect size reported: p-values reported for main effects and interactions (all p < .001 for consultation source on functional dimensions; p = .001 and p = .003 for empathy interaction); effect sizes not reported

## Author-noted limitations
- Student sample (undergraduates, mean age 18.67) may not represent real patients, especially those with stigmatized conditions
- Stigma was manipulated via disease type (AIDS vs. heart disease), not measured as a participant characteristic
- No measure of participants' prior experience with AI medical consultation
- Relational and emotional outcomes showed no consultation-source differences, which may reflect the limitations of text-based simulation rather than real clinical interactions
- Generalisability beyond simulated chat-based consultations is uncertain
