# Schilke 2025 — Methods

**Summary**: A multi-experiment program (13 experiments) using varied between-subjects online designs to test whether disclosing AI usage by human actors reduces the trust others place in those actors, with legitimacy proposed and tested as the mediating mechanism.

**Sources**: Schilke & Reimann_The transparency dilemma.pdf

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1 / RQ2 / RQ3

**Last updated**: 2026-04-19

---

## Study design
experimental (13 between-subjects online experiments; meta-analytic synthesis across experiments)

## Sample
- N: not reported for individual experiments (aggregate N across 13 experiments not specified in source page)
- Population: general public (online participants across varied task contexts)
- Domain: organizational / workplace (writing, analytics, artistic work, investment decisions)
- Country / region: not reported
- Recruitment method: online crowdsourcing (platform not specified; likely MTurk or Prolific based on context)

## Transparency operationalization
- Type: disclosure (user-level AI disclosure — actor informs audience about their own use of AI in work)
- Manipulation or measure: Between-subjects manipulation — disclosed vs. not-disclosed AI usage across various professional tasks (writing, analytics, artistic work). Framing also varied: (a) general vs. specific disclosure framing; (b) voluntary vs. mandatory disclosure; (c) third-party exposure of undisclosed AI use vs. voluntary disclosure. This operationalization is explicitly distinct from AI system transparency — it concerns human actors disclosing AI tool usage, not the AI system explaining itself.
- Scale / instrument name: not applicable (binary experimental manipulation with framing variations)
- Number of items: not applicable

## Trust operationalization
- Type: cognitive (trust perceptions) and behavioural (behavioral intentions)
- Measure: Self-reported trust perceptions in the disclosing actor and behavioural intention measures (e.g., willingness to hire, willingness to invest). Legitimacy measured as a mediating appraisal (perceived appropriateness of AI use in the professional role). Specific scale names not reported in source.
- Scale / instrument name: not reported
- Number of items: not reported
- Behavioural vs self-report: both used (trust perceptions = self-report; willingness-to-hire and willingness-to-invest = behavioural intentions)

## Moderators and covariates tested
- Technology attitudes (favorable vs. unfavorable): measured moderator — attenuates but does not eliminate the negative disclosure–trust effect; relevant to RQ3
- Perceived AI accuracy: measured moderator — higher perceived accuracy attenuates the trust penalty
- AI familiarity / prior AI usage: measured moderator — meta-analytic results show no significant attenuation of the disclosure penalty; relevant to RQ3 as a notable null finding
- Disclosure framing (general vs. specific; voluntary vs. mandatory): manipulated boundary condition — effect robust across framings
- Actor type (individual vs. organizational, e.g., investment funds): manipulated boundary condition — effect replicates across both

## Statistical approach
- Primary analysis method: between-subjects ANOVA / OLS regression per experiment; mediation analysis (for legitimacy mediation); meta-analytic synthesis across experiments
- Software: not reported
- Key model fit or effect size reported: not reported in detail in source page; meta-analytic effect sizes for moderation analyses noted (AI familiarity as non-significant moderator)
