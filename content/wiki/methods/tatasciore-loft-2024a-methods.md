## Study design
experimental (within-subjects; 2 × 2 fully crossed: transparency level × time pressure)

## Sample
- N: 64
- Population: undergraduate students
- Domain: defence / uninhabited vehicle (UV) management simulation
- Country / region: not reported (Australian university implied by author affiliations)
- Recruitment method: convenience (university undergraduate participants)

## Transparency operationalization
- Type: process transparency — SAT (Situation-Awareness Agent-Based Transparency) model levels 1–3
- Manipulation or measure: within-subjects manipulation; low transparency = table of capability weightings (SAT Level 1); high transparency = low transparency display plus bar graphs showing step-by-step UV score calculations including individual environmental factor contributions (SAT Levels 1+2+3)
- Scale / instrument name: not applicable (transparency was an interface design manipulation)
- Number of items: not applicable

## Trust operationalization
- Type: cognitive trust (self-report) and behavioural trust / appropriate reliance (signal detection performance)
- Measure: cognitive trust via 6-item Merritt (2011) questionnaire (α not reported in summary); behavioural trust via signal detection metrics — hit rate, correct rejection rate, sensitivity d', bias criterion c; usability via System Usability Scale (SUS)
- Scale / instrument name: Merritt (2011) trust questionnaire (6 items); System Usability Scale (SUS)
- Number of items: 6 (trust); 10 (SUS)
- Behavioural vs self-report: both

## Moderators and covariates tested
- Time pressure: within-subjects manipulation — low (25 s per decision) vs. high (12 s per decision); tested as moderator of transparency effects on accuracy and workload; no significant interaction found
- Note: user expertise not examined; all participants were undergraduate novices — relevant to RQ3 as a limitation

## Statistical approach
- Primary analysis method: repeated-measures ANOVA (within-subjects 2 × 2 design)
- Software: not reported
- Key model fit or effect size reported: transparency main effect on d' p = .02, η² = .09; time pressure main effect on d' η² = .44; transparency × time pressure interaction non-significant (F < 1 for most measures); high transparency increased bias c from -0.21 to -0.40, p < .001, η² = .32; trust improvement d = 0.45; usability improvement d = 0.32

## Author-noted limitations
- Sample consisted exclusively of undergraduate novices; findings may not generalise to experienced UV operators or professional military personnel
- Within-subjects design introduces potential order and carryover effects despite counterbalancing
- High transparency display increased automation bias, raising concerns about over-reliance not addressed by current design
- Time pressure levels (12 s vs. 25 s) may not fully capture the range of real-world operational time constraints
- The 80% automation reliability level is a design choice that may not reflect operational system accuracy in practice
