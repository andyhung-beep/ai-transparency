# Natural Language Explanations

**Summary**: Natural language explanations present AI reasoning in free-text prose or sentences; they are generally more accessible to non-expert users than visual or symbolic explanations, but their effectiveness depends on content quality, framing, and the agent delivering them.

**Sources**: Multiple — see individual source pages.

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1, RQ2, RQ3

**Last updated**: 2026-04-18

---

## Definition

Natural language explanations (NL explanations) express the rationale for an AI decision or prediction in ordinary written or spoken language, as opposed to visualizations, feature importance scores, or rule lists. They can be:
- **Template-based**: filling in slots with instance-specific data (e.g., "Your loan was rejected because your debt ratio of X exceeds the threshold of Y")
- **Model-generated**: produced by a language model or a dedicated text-generation system
- **Why-explanations**: answering why a decision was made
- **How-explanations**: describing how the system processed the input

## Key Findings

- Natural language explanations consistently outperform visualization-based explanations (LIME plots, feature importance charts) for non-expert users in comprehension and perceived trust (domain: software/spreadsheets; Mukhtar et al., 2023).
- NL explanations from AI agents delivered by an embodied virtual agent (with voice and gestures) produce significantly higher user trust than identical text-based explanations — with a medium effect size — suggesting the delivery medium modulates the explanation's impact on trust (domain: general/lab; Weitz, 2021).
- Generated NL explanations from a neural veracity-prediction model (E-BART) improve human accuracy in fact-checking by reducing false positives, though effects on trust are measured only implicitly (domain: NLP/fact-checking; Brand et al., 2023).
- NL explanations (GenAI) significantly improved educator feedback quality in automated grading support; by-word saliency highlights (BERT XAI) did not improve quality, suggesting NL format is important for pedagogical tasks (domain: education; Li et al., 2025).
- Why-explanations (which provide causal rationale) are preferred over how-explanations (which describe processing) by laypersons in AI symptom checkers, particularly for unfamiliar medical conditions (domain: healthcare/consumer; Woodcock et al., 2021).

## Warm vs. Competent Framing

Lim et al. (2025) examine how the *tone* of AI-generated natural language affects trust in apology contexts. Warm-toned AI apologies reduce negative emotions and improve trust through an affective route; competent-toned apologies do not compensate for AI's legitimacy deficit in the sincerity dimension. This shows that NL explanation effectiveness is sensitive to linguistic framing, not just information content (domain: general/organizational; Lim et al., 2025).

## Delivery Medium as Moderator

Weitz et al. demonstrate a linear relationship between agent human-likeness and trust: text-only NL < voice-only < embodied virtual agent. This implies that the perceived source of a natural language explanation shapes its trust effect, independently of content (domain: general/lab; Weitz, 2021).

## Limitations

- NL explanations generated by AI may be inaccurate or hallucinated, introducing new trust risks
- They are less interpretable for users who need quantitative precision (e.g., ML experts)
- Length and complexity of NL explanations must be tailored to the audience
