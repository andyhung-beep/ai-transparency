# Algorithmic Trust

**Summary**: Algorithmic trust refers to the disposition to rely on and accept decisions produced by algorithmic systems; it is shaped by explainability, perceived fairness, prior AI experience, and individual differences, and is conceptually distinct from both general trust propensity and trust in specific institutions.

**Sources**: Shin_The effects of explainability and causability on perception, trust, and acceptance.pdf; Liu 2021_In AI we trust_.pdf; Kleizen et al._Do citizens trust trustworthy artificial intelligence_.pdf

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1, RQ2, RQ3

**Last updated**: 2026-04-18

---

## Definition

Algorithmic trust is trust directed specifically at algorithms or AI systems as a class, rather than at a particular system or the institution deploying it. It is related to but distinct from:
- **General trust propensity**: dispositional tendency to trust others (a trait-level variable)
- **[[Institutional trust]]**: trust in the organisations that develop or deploy AI
- **[[Cognitive trust]]**: trust in a specific AI system's competence and reliability
- **[[Algorithm aversion]]**: a related but negatively-valenced construct (reluctance/distrust of algorithms)

Algorithmic trust is sometimes measured as a prior variable (pre-existing attitudes toward AI systems) that moderates how users respond to XAI interventions.

## Key Findings

- Algorithmic trust fully mediates the effects of [[explainability]] and [[causability]] on acceptance and use intention via perceived FAT (fairness, accountability, transparency) in a structural equation model on news recommendations (domain: e-commerce/news; Shin, 2021).
- Exposure to ML-disclosed AI (vs. human-programmed AI) significantly reduces trust and social presence, and increases uncertainty. Real transparency (genuine disclosure) partially restores trust; placebic transparency increases use intention without reducing uncertainty — through different psychological mechanisms (domain: general; Liu, 2021).
- Prior general AI trust is among the strongest predictors of citizen support for governmental AI, outperforming all ethical-AI communication treatments — suggesting algorithmic trust as a dispositional moderator of transparency effects (domain: government; Kleizen et al., 2023).
- Users with more favourable technology attitudes (a proxy for algorithmic trust) show attenuated (but not eliminated) trust penalties when AI use is disclosed by a human user (domain: organizational; Schilke & Reimann, 2025).

## Individual Differences

Algorithmic trust varies meaningfully across individuals and appears to moderate response to XAI:
- Sun et al. (2026) include algorithmic trust beliefs as a measured moderator of transparency × expectation confirmation effects on dating recommendations
- Liu (2021) measures individual differences in general AI attitudes as a moderator of ML disclosure effects

## Relationship to Algorithm Aversion

Algorithmic trust and [[algorithm-aversion]] are conceptually opposite but empirically related constructs:
- Algorithm aversion can be partially alleviated by explanations that increase understanding (Zhao; Fleiß)
- Algorithmic trust can be damaged by poor-quality explanations or disclosure of AI involvement (Schilke & Reimann; Humer)

Both constructs reflect the same underlying attitude dimension but are operationalized differently.
