# Imperfections of XAI: Phenomena Influencing AI-Assisted Decision-Making

**Source file**: [Original article](../../raw/Spitzer et al._Imperfections of XAI.pdf)

**Summary**: This study investigates how incorrect or "imperfect" XAI explanations — alongside user expertise and cognitive style — affect appropriate reliance and human–AI team performance in a bird species identification task with 136 participants.

**Sources**: Spitzer et al._Imperfections of XAI.pdf

**Research questions addressed**: RQ1, RQ2, RQ3

**Last updated**: 2026-04-18

---

## Overview

Spitzer et al. (2025) introduce the concept of "imperfect XAI" — post-hoc explanations that can be factually incorrect or misaligned with the predicted class even when the AI advice itself is correct. Published in *ACM Transactions on Interactive Intelligent Systems*, the paper proposes a research model capturing how explanation correctness, explanation modality (natural language vs. visual/example-based), user expertise, and cognitive style jointly shape appropriate reliance on AI and human–AI complementary team performance (CTP). A mixed-methods within-subjects (explanation correctness) and between-subjects (modality) design was used across 136 participants with varying levels of birding expertise. The authors also contribute a novel metric — Deception of Reliance (DoR) — to quantify the degree to which incorrect explanations mislead decision-makers.

## Key Findings

- **Incorrect explanations reduce appropriate reliance more for non-experts than for experts** (in terms of Correct AI Reliance Rate, RAIR), indicating that non-experts are more susceptible to being misled by incorrect XAI (domain: wildlife conservation / citizen science).
- **Expert users are paradoxically misled by *correct* example-based explanations when the AI advice is wrong**: experts showed significantly higher self-reliance for incorrect explanations (RSR = 0.57) than for correct ones (RSR = 0.29) in the example-based condition, suggesting correct explanations can falsely validate incorrect AI advice for experienced users (domain: wildlife conservation).
- **Example-based explanations produce higher deception of reliance (DoR) than natural language explanations** for both experts (DoR = 0.32 vs. 0.11) and non-experts (DoR = 0.26 vs. 0.06), meaning the correctness of visual/example-based explanations has a larger impact on decision behavior (domain: wildlife conservation).
- **Experts achieved complementary team performance (CTP)** — outperforming both human and AI alone — for both explanation modalities, while non-experts did not reach CTP but substantially improved their accuracy when paired with AI (domain: wildlife conservation).
- **Cognitive style moderates the effect of explanation modality on team performance**: verbal-style users are most harmed by incorrect natural language explanations (12.5% performance drop), while participants with both visual and verbal styles are least affected (domain: wildlife conservation).
- **Visual cognitive style positively predicts RAIR** — participants who process visual information well rely more correctly on AI, consistent with the visual nature of the task (domain: wildlife conservation).
- Non-experts have a higher RAIR overall than experts; experts rely more on their own initial decisions (higher RSR), which aligns with domain expertise providing a strong prior for self-reliance (domain: wildlife conservation).

## Transparency Constructs

The paper operationalizes transparency through two **explanation modalities**:

- **Natural language explanations** ([[natural-language-explanations]]): Text descriptions generated by a language model describing the predicted class (e.g., "this is a bird with a yellow belly..."). Conceptualized as a form of [[explainability]] aimed at making AI predictions interpretable to lay users.
- **Example-based explanations** ([[example-based-explanations]]): The three most visually similar training images to the query image, retrieved via cosine similarity. These function as case-based or analogical transparency.

The paper adds a novel dimension to both: **explanation correctness (fidelity/faithfulness)**, defined as whether the explanation aligns with the AI's predicted class. This connects to [[causability]] and [[traceability]] concerns — explanations may be locally misleading regardless of whether the model prediction is right. The study treats [[process-transparency]] as subordinate to this question of explanation quality.

## Trust Constructs

The paper operationalizes trust behaviorally rather than attitudinally:

- **[[behavioural-trust]]** (reliance): Measured through appropriate reliance metrics — Correct AI Reliance Rate (RAIR) and Correct Self-Reliance Rate (RSR) — following Schemmer et al. (2023). Reliance is the primary dependent variable; self-report trust attitudes are not collected.
- **[[appropriate-reliance]]**: The central normative construct. CTP (Complementary Team Performance) serves as an outcome metric capturing whether the human–AI team performs better than either alone.
- **[[trust-calibration]]**: Implicit throughout — the study is fundamentally about calibrating reliance to match actual AI accuracy and explanation fidelity.
- **[[algorithm-aversion]]**: Discussed as a risk when explanation fidelity is low and non-experts over-rely or under-rely inappropriately.

## Relevance to Research Questions

**RQ1**: Contributes nuanced operationalizations of both XAI transparency (as explanation modality × correctness) and reliance-based trust. Introduces Deception of Reliance (DoR) as a quantitative measure linking explanation quality to trust miscalibration. Trust is operationalized behaviourally, not through self-report scales (source: Spitzer et al._Imperfections of XAI.pdf).

**RQ2**: Provides strong empirical evidence of non-linear and conditional effects: (a) high-fidelity example-based explanations *decrease* expert self-reliance when AI is wrong (a deceptive but counterintuitive pattern); (b) the modality × correctness interaction differs across expertise levels; (c) natural language explanations show weaker deception effects than visual ones. These conditional effects challenge simple transparency-promotes-trust assumptions (source: Spitzer et al._Imperfections of XAI.pdf).

**RQ3**: Directly addresses user expertise as a moderator. The expertise × explanation correctness interaction on RAIR is statistically significant; expert and non-expert users are deceived differently by the same imperfect explanations. Cognitive style (verbal vs. visual) further moderates the modality effect on RSR and team performance, extending the user-characteristic analysis beyond domain expertise (source: Spitzer et al._Imperfections of XAI.pdf).
